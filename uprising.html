The 1967 Newark Uprising

In July 1967, five days of unrest shook Newark. Residents often call it the Newark Rebellion, and historians treat it as a turning point that exposed years of frustration over housing, policing, jobs, and political power.

What Happened

The unrest began on July 12, 1967, after police arrested and beat John Smith, a Black taxi driver, and word spread through the Central Ward. Over the next several days there were protests, fires, and looting. The governor sent in the New Jersey State Police and the National Guard. By the time order returned on July 17, 26 people had died, hundreds were injured, and more than a thousand had been arrested.

Underlying Causes

Many residents pointed to conditions that had built up for years. The city government did not reflect its growing Black and Latino population, police practices were a constant source of anger, and plans to clear Central Ward land for a medical and dental school added to fears of displacement.

  • Poor housing and urban renewal that displaced families
  • High unemployment as industry left the city
  • Limited political representation for Black residents
  • Tense relations between residents and the police

Aftermath

President Lyndon Johnson created the Kerner Commission after the unrest in Newark and Detroit, and its report warned that the nation was moving toward two societies, separate and unequal. In 1970 Kenneth Gibson was elected mayor, becoming the first Black mayor of a major Northeast city. Many Newark residents also left the city in the years that followed, and recovery took decades.
