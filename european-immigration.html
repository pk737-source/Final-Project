European Immigration & Industrialization

Newark began in 1666 as a small Puritan settlement on the Passaic River. Over the next two centuries it became an industrial city, and the people who arrived from Europe supplied much of the labor that made that growth possible.

A Settlement Becomes a City

Puritan families from the Connecticut colony, led by Robert Treat, founded Newark in 1666. Early residents farmed and made goods such as leather and cider. The Morris Canal, which opened in 1831, and later the railroads connected Newark to coal fields, other cities, and the port of New York, and the town was incorporated as a city in 1836.

Industry Draws Newcomers

Newark became known for leather, jewelry, brewing, carriages, and later a wide range of manufactured goods. Inventor Seth Boyden developed new methods for making malleable iron and patent leather there. Insurance also took root: the Prudential Insurance Company was founded in Newark in 1875.

Factory work pulled in immigrants in several waves. Germans and Irish arrived in large numbers in the middle of the 1800s. In the decades around 1900, Italians, Poles, Eastern European Jews, and Portuguese settlers followed.

  • German and Irish families settled early and shaped Newark's churches, breweries, and trades
  • Italian communities grew in the First Ward and other neighborhoods
  • Jewish families built businesses and congregations, especially in the Weequahic and Prince Street areas
  • The Ironbound, ringed by rail lines, became a home for working families near the factories

Why This Matters Today

By 1930 Newark's population had passed 440,000, the highest it has ever been. The neighborhoods, churches, food, and businesses that immigrants established still show up across the city, and the Ironbound remains a center of Portuguese and Brazilian life. This era set up the industrial economy whose later decline is covered on the pages that follow.
