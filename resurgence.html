Newark's Resurgence

After decades of decline, Newark has been growing again. New investment downtown, a revived arts scene, and the arrival of new communities have changed how the city looks and who lives there.

Rebuilding Downtown

The New Jersey Performing Arts Center opened in 1997 and helped draw visitors back to the city center. The Prudential Center arena opened in 2007, and new offices, housing, parks along the Passaic River, and restaurants have followed. Universities such as NJIT, Rutgers University-Newark, and Essex County College bring thousands of students into the city every day.

New and Growing Communities

Newark's population grew from about 277,000 in 2010 to about 312,000 in 2020. Today the city is home to Black, Latino, and white residents along with growing communities from the Caribbean, Latin America, Portugal, Brazil, and West Africa. Neighborhoods like the Ironbound and the North Ward show how new arrivals continue to shape the city's food, businesses, and traditions.

  • Dominican, Ecuadorian, Puerto Rican, and Colombian communities
  • Portuguese and Brazilian businesses along Ferry Street
  • West African and Caribbean residents and shops
  • Students and young professionals drawn by downtown growth

Challenges Ahead

Growth brings new questions. Rising rents raise concerns about who can afford to stay, and the city has had to respond to serious public health problems such as lead in drinking water, which led to a large program to replace lead service lines. Newark's story shows that choices made in one era have long-lasting effects, which is why paying attention to your own community matters.
